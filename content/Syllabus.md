# Aims

Why should you invest time/effort into learning about digital technology and developing new skills? Because there are too many things in life that now involve--dare I say "require"--using digital technology.

How many days in a row could you go without using at least one of your digital devices to get some task or personal business done? Could you go a week with your desktop/laptop, smartphone, and table all turned off?

How frequently do get stuck/confused/hesitant/uncertain/frustrated when doing things with your digital devices? Most times (ie multiple times per week), more than once a month? Do you think you are getting value from your digital devices that is commensurate with the monetary costs you pay for them (both cost of the devices themselves and fees you pay for software and services?)
# Objectives

1.   To explain the foundational technology of the digital domain: hardware, software, the Internet, and the Cloud
	1. inventory of "components", their purpose/role, how they work (especially/in particular in conjunction with the rest of the "stack")
	2. to give you a **pegboard** knowledge framework for understanding of digital technology
2. To enable you to think critically when
    1. you get stuck / are venturing into new territory in the digital landscape
    2. you consume content (especially from sites/services on the Internet)
    3. deciding what's right/best for you when faced with options/choices
3. To bolster your ability/proficiency with fundamental/basic "hands on" skills
    1. interacting with your device(s) -- ie, user interface (UI)
    2. organizing and finding your digital assets
    3. accessing/navigating the web effectively/
4. Digital Literacy

# Policies

- Policies intended to benefit student learning, like an attendance policy, may be perceived as punitive. Telling students that they will miss rich in-class discussions if they are not in attendance helps them directly connect the policy with learning.
